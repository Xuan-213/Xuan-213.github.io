AI-Driven Interactive LearningGame Design

Many elves live in a magical treehouse. What are they up to today? How can we help them decorate the treehouse home? This project transforms mundane tidying into an enchanting fantasy adventure. By integrating physical toys with AI-driven digital storytelling, children are invited into a "magical treehouse" to help elves decorate their home. The system weaves organization tasks into imaginative plots through AI guidance, turning storage into an act of creative play.

Anti-Competitive Observation Framework

In its data dimension, the system follows anti-competitive design principles: it observes only children's organizational habits and interests without passing judgment, and manages data hierarchically to meet developmental needs from multiple perspectives. All data serves solely as an observational reference for creative activities, with a clear statement that it must not be used to evaluate children's abilities or developmental progress. Even the analysis of expression clarity displays only ranges, never precise scores.

Privacy-First Design and Self-Transcendence

The core philosophy is privacy-first: no peer-to-peer rankings, so as to protect children's intrinsic motivation. Competition is replaced with surpassing one's past self, safeguarding children's mental well-being and reinforcing their inner drive for free creation. This design ensures every child grows at their own pace, free from the pressure of external comparison.

This is an interactive learning system centered on interest-based teaching, unfolding through themed stories such as "Birthday of the Pig" and "The Egg's Dream." Children naturally complete tidying and organizing tasks while helping little elves decorate a treehouse. The experience is guided by an animated map in a level-based quest format, subtly weaving cognitive inspiration into gameplay so that children absorb knowledge naturally through task completion, rather than passively receiving instruction.

The system emphasizes transforming functional instructions into warm, emotional communication. Through emotional feedback, it internalizes motivation — shifting children from "I have to do this" to "I want to do this." Children can keep multiple electronic spirits as pets and switch between them by clicking. This enduring sense of emotional connection and companionship further strengthens learning initiative and engagement.
